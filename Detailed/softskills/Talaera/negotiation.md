Language for preparing a negotiation - 
Lets begin some basic language for preparing and planning your next nefotiation. When preparing for a negotiation.

Questions about you - 
1. What do we want from this negotiation?
2. What are our advantages in this negotiations?
3. On the other hand, what are our disadvantages in this negotiation?
4. What do we have that the other part wants or needs?


Questions about the other party -
1. What is there experince in this industry/on this topic?
2. What are thier interests? How are those interests priortized


Questions about logistics : 

1. Where and when should this negotiation take place?
2. How long should negotiation talks last?
3. What deadlines are we facing?

Formal English:

is polite and respectful
has proper grammar and complete sentences
is less direct and less emotional
uses proper titles and last names
Informal English: 

is short and direct 
is more emotional and casual
uses slang, abbreviations, and acronyms

Basic tips to be more persuasive
Persuasion is about convincing someone to see things your way. Even with simple language, you can be effective in making your point! Here are some essential tips you can apply to be more persuasive.

Be clear: Use simple sentences.
Be positive: Focus on good reasons and benefits.
Listen: Understand the other person's side.



Useful phrases for simple persuasion
Introducing your idea:
  I think we should...
  What if we try...
Giving reasons:
  It's a good idea because...
  This way, we can...
Asking for agreement:
  Don't you think?
  Wouldn't it be better if...?
  
Addressing concerns:
  I understand your point, and...
  Yes, although if we...

Encouraging a decision:
  Shall we give it a go?  
  Why not try it and see?



Asking questions during a negotiation

Now that you have learned a few techniques and phrases for understanding the situation and building rapport, let's dive into our first technique for the actual negotiation.  At the beginning of a negotiation, or during preliminary meetings, it is helpful to thoroughly understand the other party's needs and goals. By asking questions, you can:

Find out their needs and goals

Determine their understanding of the situation and interest in it
Get an idea of their behavior and communication style
Types of questions:
Questions are generally divided into two categories: open and closed.

Closed questions - can only be answered with a 'yes' or 'no' but can be a very effective way to set boundaries.
Open questions - can help you learn more information and more about the other party's communication and behavioral styles.
